## 1. Sidewalk snow-clearing bylaw and enforcement

### The rule
- Property owners must clear snow and ice down to bare pavement on the public sidewalk next to their property **within 24 hours after snowfall ends**. The cleared path must be at least **1.5 m wide**. Source: Street Bylaw 20M88, s.67. [VERIFIED] https://www.calgary.ca/bylaws/snow-ice.html and https://www.calgary.ca/csps/abs/bylaws-by-topic/snow-shovelling.html
- You can only file a 311 complaint once **24 hours have passed since the last snowfall anywhere in the city**. [VERIFIED] https://www.calgary.ca/csps/abs/bylaws-by-topic/snow-shovelling.html
- Bylaw 20M88 is still the governing bylaw as of February 2026. Owners cover about 90% of sidewalks and the City about 10%. [VERIFIED] https://www.calgary.ca/council/ward-2/articles/february-19--2026--snow-update-for-ward-2.html

### Enforcement process after a 311 complaint
1. 311 assigns the complaint a file number and forwards it to a **peace officer**.
2. Complaints are **triaged by public-safety risk**.
3. City wording: "Due to the high volume of complaints registered and limited City resources, it can take **several days** before an initial visit occurs." [VERIFIED] https://www.calgary.ca/bylaws/snow-ice.html
   - **This is the City's own statement of the bottleneck CivicSignal targets.**
4. If the sidewalk is still not cleared, the officer leaves a **City Notice / warning** with a compliance deadline. [VERIFIED] https://www.calgary.ca/bylaws/snow-ice.html
5. The officer returns about **24 hours later**. [UNVERIFIED, CBC 2018 via search snippet; CBC returned 403] https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/calgary-snow-removal-1.4472100
6. If the sidewalk is still not done, a **City contractor clears it** and the owner is invoiced. [VERIFIED] https://www.calgary.ca/bylaws/snow-ice.html

### Fees and fines
All from https://www.calgary.ca/bylaws/snow-ice.html [VERIFIED].

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Contractor clearing invoice | **$150 + GST minimum flat rate, plus an admin fee**. Added to the property tax bill if unpaid |
| Fine, 1st offence | **$250** |
| Fine, 2nd offence | **$750** |
| Fine, 3rd and later offences within 12 months | **$750** each. Repeat tickets may require a court appearance |
| Unpaid fines | Collected at driver's licence or registration renewal |

- Older schedule (late 2019): $250 / $500 / $750. Source: Global News, 3 Jan 2021. [VERIFIED] https://globalnews.ca/news/7552568/calgary-sidewalk-snow-removal

  - Older reporting says "over five thousand complaints in a winter". [UNVERIFIED, old Global News] https://globalnews.ca/news/1047793/snowy-sidewalks-irk-pedestrians-prompting-fines/

## 2. Roads snow and ice control (Mobility / "Winter Maintenance")

### Priority Snow Plan
All from https://Calgary.ca/roads/conditions/sanding-plowing-priorities.html [VERIFIED].

| Priority | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | More than 20,000 vehicles/day (Crowchild, Memorial, Macleod), plus downtown roads and cycle tracks | **0 to 18 h** |
| **P2** | 5,000 or more vehicles/day: community and transit routes, signalized intersections, emergency and bus routes, on-street bike lanes | **18 to 36 h** |
| **P3** | Lower-volume routes: clearing windrows at crosswalks and **wheelchair ramps**, turn lanes, ramps, connecting bus stops | **36 h +** |
| Pedestrian | **More than 750 km of prioritized pathways**, sidewalks next to City property, bridges, LRT platforms, **about 1,300 high-use bus stops** | **0 to 24 h** |
| Residential | "The City does not typically clear snow from residential streets and works to maintain a hard snow pack." Crews level ruts and sand/salt when needed | none |

- If another snow event starts before the plan finishes, **the plan resets to Day One**. [VERIFIED] 2023/24 annual report, linked below.
- The 18 h / 36 h targets were set by Council in the **Winter Maintenance Policy CP2024-06**, approved 28 May 2024. The old targets were 24 h / 48 h. [VERIFIED] https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/transportation/roads/documents/road-maintenance/snow-and-ice-control/snow-and-ice-control-annual-report-2023-2024.pdf
- The City clears about **10% of Calgary's roughly 5,000 km of sidewalks**. [10% VERIFIED on the calgary.ca Ward 2 page above; the 5,000 km figure is from a Global News snippet, UNVERIFIED] https://globalnews.ca/news/9507863/more-inclusive-snow-removal-calgary/amp
- Back lanes and engineered walkways are not cleared. Residents report problems through 311. [VERIFIED] https://calgary.ca/roads/conditions/snow-ice-faqs.html

### Recommended pitch numbers [DERIVED]
The benefits load is an assumption. I found no published City of Calgary benefits rate. 1.30x is a common, conservative public-sector figure.

- **Bylaw/peace officer:** $36.69 to $52.00 × 1.30 is about $48 to $68 per hour. Add about $10 to $15 per hour for the vehicle (assumption). **Use about $65 per hour, fully loaded.**
- **Roads operator, labour only:** $31.05 × 1.30 is about **$40 per hour**.
- **Top-down check from the City's own 2025/26 report:**
  - Staffing 35% of $60.7M is about $21.2M across 420 staff, or about $50k per person per season.
  - At about 1,200 season hours each (assumption: 7 months), that is about $42 per hour.
  - Staffing plus equipment (62%) is about $37.6M, or about **$75 per operator-hour including the truck**.
- **Pitch math:** each avoided wasted officer visit is about 0.5 to 1 h of drive, inspect and notice time, so roughly **$30 to $65 per visit avoided**. Each hour of rerouted plow time is worth about **$75**.

## 4. Fall injuries on ice

### Calgary emergency departments
- **14,977** ED visits for falls on ice and snow (ICD-10 W00) at Calgary's 4 adult hospitals, Jan 2008 to Dec 2018. That is **about 1,360 per year** [DERIVED].
  - January (3,581), February (2,997) and March (2,954) account for more than half.
  - Women made up 57%.
  - Over age 50, women had about 25% more visits than men.
  - On 16 March 2017, a freeze-thaw event caused **3x the average daily ED visits**.
  - Source: Tarcea et al., "The Slip and Fall Index", *Canadian Journal of Public Health*, 2024. [VERIFIED] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11027758/
- **Alberta hospitalizations from falls on ice are about 3x Ontario's** (same paper). [VERIFIED]
- Alberta averaged **42.3 hospitalizations per 100,000 people** for ice and snow falls, 2011 to 2016. [UNVERIFIED, CBC snippet, 403] https://www.cbc.ca/1.4460651
- Alberta Health Services (AHS): **About 11,000 adults aged 65+ were hospitalized for falls in Alberta in 2022** (all causes, not only ice). [VERIFIED] https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/news/wa/ne-wa-2024-12-16-preventing-slips-and-falls.docx
- AHS and Calgary EMS: "hundreds" of winter fall-related ED visits and "dozens" of EMS slip-and-fall calls (Dec 2024). [VERIFIED] https://globalnews.ca/news/10913919/calgary-ice-slippery-streets-sidewalks

### Canada-wide (Public Health Agency of Canada, PHAC)
Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/publications/healthy-living/surveillance-report-falls-older-adults-canada.html [VERIFIED]
- **18% of older adults' falls happen on ice or snow.**
- Fall hospitalizations among older adults: **72,392 in 2019/20**, up 47% since 2008/09.
- 34% of those involved a hip fracture.
- Fall stays run **4 to 5 days longer** than average.
- Direct cost of seniors' falls: **$5.6 billion** (2018).
- In Alberta and Ontario, seniors' fall ED visits rose from 129,825 to 189,679 between 2010/11 and 2019/20.

### Parachute, *Cost of Injury in Canada 2021* (2018 data)
Source: https://parachute.ca/en/news-release/injuries-divert-essential-hospital-resources-and-cost-canadian-residents-29-4-billion-annually/ [VERIFIED]
- All injuries cost **$29.4B** per year.
- **Falls cost $10.3B, or 35% of the total**, more than any other cause.
- Injuries cause 231,530 hospitalizations and 4.6M ED visits per year.

### Cost per fall hospitalization
- **$20,500 average hospital admission cost per fall** and an average stay of about 3 weeks. Alberta seniors' falls cost **$290M** in 2021. Source: Alberta Finding Balance campaign (Injury Prevention Centre), via local news. [UNVERIFIED: the article URL now returns 404] https://www.rimbeyreview.com/community/finding-balance-campaign-designed-to-raise-awareness-6645478
- A matched-cohort study found a seriously injured faller cost **$44,203** versus $13,507 for a non-faller, about **$30.7k extra**. This was a small sample (37 cases). [UNVERIFIED secondary] https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation/ispor-13th-annual-international-meeting/the-direct-costs-of-injurious-falls-in-seniors
- Canadian Institute for Health Information (CIHI) Cost of a Standard Hospital Stay: Alberta is about **$9,341**, the highest of the provinces. [UNVERIFIED, search snippet; the CIHI page loads data dynamically] https://www.cihi.ca/en/indicators/cost-of-a-standard-hospital-stay
- **Pitch-safe framing:** about $20k per fall admission in Alberta, and a hip-fracture admission is "$20,000+ in acute care alone". Osteoporosis Canada FLS toolkit: https://fls.osteoporosis.ca/wp-content/uploads/FLS-TOOLKIT-App-B-1-1.pdf [UNVERIFIED snippet]
