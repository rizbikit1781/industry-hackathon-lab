# Hail SME Reference: Predicting Severe-Hail Days in the Calgary Area from ERA5

Scope: 48-h hackathon model, target = "severe hail (>=20 mm) reported in the Calgary box today", predictors = ERA5 morning state (+ Open-Meteo surface history), labels = ECCC Integrated Canadian Hail Database (ICHD) 2005–2022. Every number below is cited; where I computed something from the ICHD CSV myself it is marked **[computed from ICHD v1.0.0]**.

---

## 1. Physics that matters for prediction

- **Updraft strength.** Hail grows while suspended in an updraft; max updraft speed scales as sqrt(2*CAPE), which is why CAPE (and CAPE x shear) dominate every hail index. Shear widens the updraft and lengthens hail trajectories in the growth zone, so larger shear -> larger hail (Kumjian & Lombardo 2020, cited in [Taszarek et al. 2020](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/31691)).
- **Supercooled water and the hail growth zone (HGZ).** Growth occurs mainly where cloud temperature is -10 to -30 C; the depth of that layer (THKHGZ) is an explicit term in the Large Hail Parameter ([Johnson & Sugden 2014](https://ejssm.org/archives/2014/vol-9-5-2014/)).
- **Freezing level (FZL) and wet-bulb zero (WBZ).** Hail melts below WBZ. NWS: "WBZ heights between 7000 ft and 10,500 ft AGL correlate well with large hail"; too high = large melting layer, too low = atmosphere too cool/stable ([NWS glossary](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=WBZ)). SHIP down-weights profiles with FZL < 2400 m ([SPC](https://www.spc.noaa.gov/exper/soundings/help/ship.html)). Mid-level dry air lowers WBZ via evaporative cooling.
- **Why Alberta / Hailstorm Alley.** (i) Elevation (~1000–1100 m) puts the freezing level closer to the ground, so stones fall through a shorter melting layer; (ii) orographic/upslope lift on the foothills triggers storms; (iii) cropland evapotranspiration supplies low-level moisture ([Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/weather/forecasts/brittain-the-science-behind-canadas-hail-alley-alberta-red-deer)). Hail/thunderstorm ratio increases with elevation across Canada (r^2 = 0.78 all hail, 0.65 severe; [Kehler et al. 2025](https://journalofcrr.com/research/03-06-kehler-desorcy/)). Smith & Yau's Alberta outbreak model: upper ridge over the AB/SK border, trough over central BC, SW flow aloft with divergence over the Rockies; plus the foothills **mountain–plain circulation** and the **Alberta dryline** as initiation mechanisms; forecasting hinges on when/where the capping lid is removed ([UNSTABLE 2008 overview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258488373_The_Understanding_Severe_Thunderstorms_and_Alberta_Boundary_Layers_Experiment_UNSTABLE_2008)). Canadian hail-hour/hail-day maximum sits along the lee of the Rockies in Alberta ([Brunet & Brimelow 2024](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/apme/63/10/JAMC-D-23-0214.1.xml)).

## 2. Predictive ingredients and indices

ERA5 CDS dataset names: `reanalysis-era5-single-levels` and `reanalysis-era5-pressure-levels`. ERA5-native single-level fields relevant here (CDS long name / short name): `convective_available_potential_energy`/cape, `convective_inhibition`/cin, `k_index`/kx, `total_totals_index`/totalx, `zero_degree_level`/deg0l, `total_column_water_vapour`/tcwv, `boundary_layer_height`/blh, `2m_temperature`, `2m_dewpoint_temperature`, `10m_u_component_of_wind`, `10m_v_component_of_wind`, `surface_pressure`, `mean_sea_level_pressure`, `convective_precipitation`, `total_precipitation` ([ERA5 variable table](https://ecmwf-models.readthedocs.io/en/stable/variables_era5.html)). Pressure-level fields: `temperature`, `u_component_of_wind`, `v_component_of_wind`, `geopotential`, `relative_humidity`, `specific_humidity`, `vertical_velocity`, `divergence`.

Important: **ERA5 `cape` is effectively a most-unstable CAPE** — the IFS lifts parcels from every model level up to 350 hPa (30-hPa mixed layers in the lowest 60 hPa, no pure surface parcel) and keeps the maximum ([ECMWF CAPE overview](https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2019/19278-overview-convective-available-potential-energy-and-convective-inhibition-provided-nwp-models.pdf), [ECMWF 47r3 note](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/spaces/FCST/pages/233248178/47r3+new+parameters+CAPE+and+CIN)). SB/ML CAPE must be computed from pressure-level profiles (MetPy `most_unstable_cape_cin`, `mixed_layer_cape_cin`, `surface_based_cape_cin`).

Thresholds below are North American operational values unless noted. Taszarek et al. caution that SPC-calibrated thresholds (SHIP, SCP) transfer poorly outside the US; **re-tune on your own data**.

| 0–1 km shear, 0–3 km SRH | Low-level rotation; mainly tornado discriminators | Weak hail skill vs 0–6 km; Prein & Holland used 0–3 km shear + SRH as 2 of 4 predictors ([P&H 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212094718300744)). | PL winds (1000–700 hPa), need storm motion (Bunkers) for SRH |

| Lifted index / Showalter | Parcel instability at 500 hPa | <= -7 / <= -8 "extremely unstable" (NOAA [JetStream](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/upperair/severe-weather)); SI uses the 850 hPa parcel, which matters at 1000 m elevation (850 hPa is ~500 m AGL here). | derived from PL |
| K-index | Airmass-thunderstorm moisture/lapse; **not** a severity index | 26–30 = 40–60 %, 31–35 = 60–80 % thunderstorm probability ([JetStream](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/upperair/severe-weather)) | `k_index` (native) |
| Total Totals = T850 + Td850 - 2*T500 | 850–500 lapse + 850 moisture | >= 50 severe likely ([JetStream](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/upperair/severe-weather)); TT and VT were among the 7 selected predictors in the German ERA5 CNN hail model ([Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2025.1527391/full)) | `total_totals_index` (native) |
| SWEAT | TT + 850 Td + 850/500 wind speed and veering | > 300 severe possible, > 400 tornadic ([JetStream](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/upperair/severe-weather)) | derived from PL 850/500 |
| 700–500 hPa lapse rate | Mid-level steepness -> CAPE in HGZ | SHIP scales down below 5.8 K/km; LHP centred on 6.5 K/km ([SPC](https://www.spc.noaa.gov/exper/soundings/help/ship.html), [J&S 2014](https://ejssm.org/archives/2014/vol-9-5-2014/)) | PL `temperature`, `geopotential` at 700, 500 |
| 500 hPa temperature | Cold mid-levels; SHIP term | enters SHIP as (-T500) | PL `temperature` 500 |
| Freezing level height | Melting depth; SHIP term | SHIP scaled down when FZL < 2400 m; one of P&H 2018's 4 predictors | `zero_degree_level` (native) or derived |
| WBZ height | Melting incl. evaporation | 7,000–10,500 ft AGL (2.1–3.2 km) favourable; >90 % of hail with WBZ 5,000–12,000 ft; largest near 9,000 ft ([NWS](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=WBZ)). AR-HAIL's 4th predictor ([Battaglioli 2023](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/23/3651/2023/)). | derive wet-bulb profile from PL T, RH, p |
| Precipitable water / low-level mixing ratio | Moisture supply; "ample moisture a necessary ingredient" for >=5 cm hail ([Taszarek 2020](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/31691)) | SHIP scaled down if MU mixing ratio < 11 g/kg (likely rarely met in Alberta — re-tune) | `tcwv`; MU mixing ratio from PL q |
| Dewpoint depression / mid-level dry air | Entrainment, downdrafts, lower WBZ | 700 hPa T–Td; mean RH 850–500 is an AR-CHaMo lightning predictor | PL `relative_humidity` 700, 500 |
| HGZ (-10 to -30 C) depth | Growth layer thickness | LHP term (3200 - THKHGZ)/500 | derived from PL T + geopotential |
| SHIP | Composite for >= 2 in (51 mm) hail | > 1 favourable, 1.5–2.0 typical when sig hail reported, > 4 very high ([SPC](https://www.spc.noaa.gov/exper/soundings/help/ship.html)) | derived: MUCAPE, MU mixing ratio, LR 700–500, T500, 0–6 km shear |
| LHP | >= 89 mm vs < 51 mm hail; adds HGZ depth, EL-level shear, storm-relative wind directions | 0 if BWD < 14 m/s or MUCAPE < 400 J/kg ([J&S 2014](https://ejssm.org/archives/2014/vol-9-5-2014/)) | derived; needs full wind profile to EL |

## 3. Evidence from ERA5 hail studies

- **Taszarek et al. 2020 (ERA5, 92,323 hail reports, US + Europe).** Severity rises with CAPE and shear; WMAXSHEAR captures most of the signal; effective shear is the best single shear metric for >=5 cm hail; LFC and CIN distinguish hail sizes better than LCL; low-level moisture is a necessary ingredient. Favourable environments across Europe occur 1400–2100 UTC ([paper](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/31691)).
- **Battaglioli et al. 2023 (AR-CHaMo / ARhail).** Hail = P(thunderstorm) x P(hail | storm). Lightning model predictors: MUCAPE, 925–500 hPa bulk shear, MU mixing ratio at 500 hPa (MU500_MIX), mean RH 850–500, specific humidity at 925 hPa. Conditional hail (>= 2 cm) predictors: **MUCAPE, 925–500 hPa shear, MU500_MIX, WBZ height**. Reports gridded to 0.25 deg, hourly, 3-h verification windows. AUC > 0.95 at 60 h lead, 0.86 at 180 h; beats CAPE x shear at all leads and SHIP at short leads ([NHESS](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/23/3651/2023/)).
- **Prein & Holland 2018 (ERA-Interim, > 2.5 cm hail).** Four predictors — instability, freezing-level height, 0–3 km shear, SRH — give skilful regional hail frequencies; outperform standard indices; **deficiencies "in regions with strong orographic forcing and low shear environments"** — i.e. exactly the foothills ([WCE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212094718300744)).
- **German ERA5 CNN hail model (2005–2019, 12 UTC fields).** Seven selected predictors: CAPESHEAR (dominant), q850, 6-km wind speed, u500, bulk shear, VT, TT. Skill for large events: POD 0.78, FAR 0.30, CSI 0.58, HSS 0.66, accuracy 88 % ([Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2025.1527391/full)). 18 UTC performed similarly to 12 UTC.
- **Canada.** Brunet & Brimelow 2024 built the ICHD and a lightning-proxy climatology; national hail-day maximum along the Alberta foothills ([JAMC](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/apme/63/10/JAMC-D-23-0214.1.xml)). ECCC also published **ERA5 extracted for all 2,092 ICHD hail events** (single-level + 20 pressure levels 300–1000 hPa, hourly, events = reports clustered within 7 h x 256 km) — useful as a positives sanity check ([Zenodo 10041843](https://zenodo.org/records/10041843)). A Canada-wide ERA5 downburst ML study (38 parameters, 306 vs 19,132 cases) got ~89 % cross-validated accuracy with logistic regression / RF / GBM; MUCAPE, MUCIN, WMAX, WMAXSHEAR and EL best characterised hail environments ([search summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333179454_Application_of_machine_learning_to_large_hail_prediction_-_The_importance_of_radar_reflectivity_lightning_occurrence_and_convective_parameters_derived_from_ERA5)). Hanesiak et al. 2024 did ERA5-based significant-tornado environments for Canada 1980–2020 ([JGR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2023JD040614)).
- **HAILCAST (Brimelow).** 1-D cloud + hail-growth model driven by a proximity sounding; skilful for max hail size in Alberta when given a representative sounding ([Brimelow et al. 2002](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/17/5/1520-0434_2002_017_1048_mmhsia_2_0_co_2.xml), [2006](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/21/2/waf915_1.xml)). Not needed for a 48-h hackathon, but shows a single morning profile carries real size information.
- **ERA5 CAPE bias.** ERA5 **underestimates CAPE (and to a lesser degree CIN), low-level moisture and wind shear, especially in the extremes**, versus rawinsondes ([Taszarek et al. 2021, npj](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41612-021-00190-x); [J. Climate ERA5/MERRA-2 comparison](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10227081)). Using pressure-level rather than model-level data adds a further 20–30 J/kg low bias in monthly-mean MLCAPE ([Taszarek 2021 search summary](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10227081)). Practical: use ranks/quantiles or tree models; do not trust absolute SPC thresholds.
- **Realistic skill.** Day-level hail/no-hail on a single metro box with ~10 positive days per summer: expect AUC ~0.80–0.90 and CSI ~0.3–0.5; the ARhail AUC > 0.95 is for gridded, hourly, Europe-wide data and will not transfer. HSS 0.66 / CSI 0.58 (Germany, country-wide area target) is an upper bound.

## 4. Alberta / Calgary specifics

- **Diurnal timing.** Hail is a "late-afternoon or evening" phenomenon ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/hail)); the Alberta Hail Project launched its hail-day sounding at 1715 LT from Penhold ([Alberta Hail Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Hail_Project)). **[computed from ICHD v1.0.0]** severe (>= 20 mm) reports in the Calgary box (50.4–51.7 N, 113.2–114.9 W), converting UTC to MDT (UTC-6): peak 15–19 LT (16 LT and 18 LT each 60 reports, 17 LT 52), 91 % between 13 and 21 LT; all-Alberta severe peak 16–18 LT. The two billion-dollar storms hit Calgary 1630–1930 MDT (2020) and 1945–2030 MDT (2024).
  -> **"Morning state" = 12 UTC (06 MDT)**; 18 UTC (12 MDT) is a legitimate "noon update" and performed as well as 12 UTC in Germany; 00 UTC (18 MDT) is during the event, so only usable as a diagnostic, not a forecast.
- **Seasonality.** Favourable conditions begin in spring, peak in July–August ([Etkin, ICLR](https://www.iclr.org/wp-content/uploads/PDFS/canada-s-hail-climatology.pdf)). **[computed]** Calgary-box severe reports by month: May 7, Jun 66, **Jul 211**, Aug 108, Sep 1. Restrict the model to May–September.
- **Frequency.** Central Alberta: ~56 hail days/yr (region-wide, any hail) ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/hail)); Calgary normalized 16.0 hail days/yr ([Kehler 2025](https://journalofcrr.com/research/03-06-kehler-desorcy/)); Hailstorm Alley "more than 40 hailstorms every summer" ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/storms-alberta-northern-hail-project-1.6487536)). **[computed]** Calgary box 2005–2022: 597 reports, 394 severe, **170 severe-hail days = 9.4/yr** (range 5–17/yr; 2005 has only 1 and is clearly incomplete), 12.7 any-hail days/yr, 2.3 severe reports per severe day.
- **Storm motion.** Storms initiate on the foothills and move east/southeast into the plains under SW flow aloft ([UNSTABLE](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258488373_The_Understanding_Severe_Thunderstorms_and_Alberta_Boundary_Layers_Experiment_UNSTABLE_2008)); the 2024 supercell formed W of Cochrane ~1715 MDT and tracked SE over N Calgary ([Western News](https://news.westernu.ca/2024/08/northern-hail-project-calgary-2024/)). Counter-example: on 13 June 2020 the 700 hPa steering flow was from 150 deg, storms moved NW/NE and merged ([CMOS](https://bulletin.cmos.ca/the-billion-dollar-calgary-hailstorm-of-13-june-2020-part-i-overview/)). Include 500/700 hPa wind direction as features; don't hard-code a direction.
- **5 Aug 2024 setup (NHP).** Modified HRRR sounding: SBCAPE only ~950 J/kg, 0–6 km BWD ~45 kt, 75-kt jet at 10–11 km, long straight hodograph; approaching upper trough + mountain–plain circulation + low-level convergence zone. "Conditions were favourable ... but there were no indications of a major event"; "an extreme event does not necessarily require extreme antecedent conditions"; models agreed on kinematics, not buoyancy. Supercell with large BWER, 76 dBZ, ZDR ~0 dB, 52 mm hail measured, swath > 120 km x 12 km, CAD 3.29 B insured ([NHP ECSS 2025](https://presentations.copernicus.org/ECSS2025/ECSS2025-87_supplement.pdf), [TWN](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/weather/severe/august-5-2024-calgary-hailstorm-was-one-of-canadas-costliest-disasters)). Lesson for the model: shear/kinematic features carry weight even when CAPE is modest; treat CAPE x shear, not CAPE alone, as primary.
- **13 June 2020 setup.** CAPE 2216 J/kg, CIN -40, airmass storms initiated along the mountain–plain circulation boundary and S Calgary, merged, softball hail, CAD 1.3 B ([CMOS](https://bulletin.cmos.ca/the-billion-dollar-calgary-hailstorm-of-13-june-2020-part-i-overview/)).

## 5. Data pitfalls (ICHD)

- **Columns** (v1.0.0 CSV, 7,000 rows): `Start Time, Year, Month, Day, Hour, Longitude, Latitude, Province Code, Reference Object, Hail Diameter (mm)` **[computed]**. Timestamps are **UTC** ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/8015925)). A 23:30 UTC report is 17:30 MDT the same day, but 00:45–06:00 UTC reports belong to the **previous** local day — convert before building day labels (a UTC-day label would split evening storms across two days).
- **Severe threshold.** ECCC severe = >= 20 mm. Coin mapping in the file **[computed]**: dime 18, penny 19, **nickel 21 (severe)**, quarter 24, loonie 26.5, toonie 28, ping pong 40, golf ball 43, hen egg 50, tennis 67, baseball 74, softball 97 mm. Note nickel (21 mm) clears the threshold but dime/penny don't; 2.3 % of rows have blank diameter (`unknown`, empty) — drop or treat as unlabeled.
- **Population bias.** Reports concentrate in urban areas; Kehler et al. deliberately used only StatCan urban areas because of it ([JCRR](https://journalofcrr.com/research/03-06-kehler-desorcy/)). Rural foothills storms that never reach Calgary are under-reported -> false "negatives". Mitigation: wide box, lightning-proxy climatology as a prior, or weight by the Brunet & Brimelow proxy.
- **Reporting inhomogeneity.** Report counts per year range 94 (2005) to 779 (2016) nationally **[computed]**; v1.1.0 authors warn the data were "not validated against external data sources" and should not be used for trends ([Zenodo v1.1.0](https://zenodo.org/records/17517119)). Don't let the model learn year.
- **Multiple reports per storm.** 2.3 severe reports per severe day in the box **[computed]**; ECCC clustered reports within 7 h x 256 km into 2,092 events ([Zenodo 10041843](https://zenodo.org/records/10041843)). Aggregate to one label per local day per box.
- **Class balance.** ~9 positive days per 153-day season (~6 %). Use stratified year-blocked CV (leave-one-year-out), not random splits.

## 6. Recommended feature list and ERA5 request

Ranked (literature weight x ERA5 reliability):

1. WMAXSHEAR / CAPESHEAR = sqrt(2*MUCAPE) x 0–6 km shear (derived)
2. ERA5 `cape` (approx MUCAPE) and derived MLCAPE/SBCAPE
3. 0–6 km bulk shear (10 m to 500 hPa or 6 km AGL); 925–500 hPa shear (ARhail form)
4. SHIP (derived) — include even though mis-calibrated; trees will re-threshold it
5. 700–500 hPa lapse rate; 500 hPa temperature
6. WBZ height (derived) and `zero_degree_level`
7. Low-level moisture: 2 m dewpoint, 850 hPa q, MU mixing ratio, `tcwv`
8. `cin` (native) — expect nonlinear response
9. `total_totals_index`, `k_index` (native, cheap)
10. 700 hPa and 500 hPa dewpoint depression (mid-level dry air)
11. HGZ depth (-10 to -30 C), LFC and EL heights (derived)
12. 500/700 hPa wind direction and speed; 250–300 hPa jet speed
13. 0–1 km shear, 0–3 km SRH (secondary)
14. `boundary_layer_height`, 2 m temperature; convective precipitation at 12/18 UTC as "already raining" flag
15. Open-Meteo surface history (free, ERA5/ERA5-Land, `timezone=America/Edmonton` returns local time; no CAPE in its hourly list ([Open-Meteo docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api))): previous-day max T, previous-day precipitation, 3-day rainfall (soil moisture proxy), overnight min dewpoint.
16. Calendar: day-of-year (sin/cos). Never year.

**Single ERA5 request (do it once):**

- Datasets: `reanalysis-era5-single-levels` and `reanalysis-era5-pressure-levels`, `product_type: reanalysis`, `format: netcdf`.
- Years 2005–2022, months 05–09, all days.
- Hours (UTC): `['00:00','06:00','12:00','18:00']` — 12 UTC is the morning forecast state, 18 UTC the noon update, 00 UTC (prior evening, 18 MDT) gives antecedent state and is also the diagnostic hour closest to the event peak; 06 UTC is cheap and gives overnight moisture. Four hours x 153 days x 18 yr is small over this box.
- Area (N, W, S, E): `[52.5, -115.5, 49.5, -112.0]` — covers Cochrane–Airdrie–Calgary–High River and the foothills initiation zone upstream; 13 x 15 ERA5 grid points at 0.25 deg. Label box can be the tighter 50.4–51.7 N, 113.2–114.9 W; features can use box max/mean of the wider area (storms initiate upstream).
- Single-level variables: `convective_available_potential_energy`, `convective_inhibition`, `k_index`, `total_totals_index`, `zero_degree_level`, `total_column_water_vapour`, `boundary_layer_height`, `2m_temperature`, `2m_dewpoint_temperature`, `10m_u_component_of_wind`, `10m_v_component_of_wind`, `surface_pressure`, `mean_sea_level_pressure`, `convective_precipitation`, `total_precipitation`, `geopotential` (surface orography, time-invariant).
- Pressure-level variables: `temperature`, `u_component_of_wind`, `v_component_of_wind`, `geopotential`, `relative_humidity`, `specific_humidity`, `vertical_velocity`.
- Pressure levels: `['1000','975','950','925','900','875','850','825','800','775','750','700','650','600','550','500','450','400','350','300','250','200']` — the lowest levels (1000–900 hPa) are underground at Calgary (~890 hPa surface) and must be masked using `surface_pressure`; 250/200 for the jet.
- Derivation stack: MetPy for parcel CAPE/CIN/LFC/EL, wet-bulb profile -> WBZ, bulk shear, Bunkers storm motion -> SRH; SHIP per SPC formula with ERA5 units (J/kg, g/kg, K/km, deg C, m/s).

## Key gaps / uncertainties

- No published Alberta-specific threshold tables for CAPE/shear/WBZ were found; Alberta values will be lower than SPC ones (elevation, cooler mid-levels) — learn them from the data.
- Prein & Holland flag reanalysis hail algorithms as weakest in orographic, low-shear settings; expect the foothills to be the hardest part of the domain.
- The ECSS 2025 Calgary case study is a conference supplement, not a peer-reviewed paper.
